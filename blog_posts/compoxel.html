Compoxel: Bypassing I/O Bottlenecks in 10-Million Entity GPU Simulations

Status Completed
Scale 10,000,000 Agents
Tech Stack
CUDA Modal Python

A high-performance physics architecture built to simulate 10 million individual entities. By stripping out Python CPU compression and utilizing raw memory-to-disk binary dumps, the system exports ~100GB of simulation data directly through data center backbones to distributed Blender rendering farms.

1. The 10-Million Entity Run

Simulating fluid-like gravitational dynamics for 10 million stars requires an engine capable of handling extreme spatial density without catastrophic warp divergence. Rendered across 100 parallel T4 cloud GPUs via Modal, the final 800-frame sequence demonstrates the Hybrid TreePM algorithm operating at maximum scale.

2. Algorithmic Scaling and Comparison

To validate the efficiency of the TreePM implementation, a 500k-entity benchmark was executed across four distinct algorithmic pathways. The $O(N^2)$ Naive approach required ~13 hours of compute time, whereas the Hybrid architecture resolved the identical 800-frame state in roughly 6 minutes.

3. Mathematical Core

The foundation of the Particle-Mesh step relies on mapping discrete particle masses onto a continuous grid, allowing us to solve the Poisson equation for the gravitational potential $\Phi$:

$$ \nabla^2 \Phi = 4 \pi G \rho $$ Poisson Equation for Gravity

By splitting the force calculation, the engine avoids the standard $N^2$ trap. Short-range forces are handled via direct $P^3M$ handshake integrations, while distant masses are treated as voxelized densities.

4. Cloud I/O Pipeline and CPU Bottleneck

Profiling the A100 revealed that execution was bound not by GPU compute, but by the CPU attempting to single-thread `ZIP_DEFLATED` compression on 120MB memory arrays every frame. The solution was to trade disk space for compute time. By switching to `ZIP_STORED`, the engine dumps raw binary directly to the NVMe SSD, bypassing the CPU entirely.
